Handheld 3D LiDAR scanner (in progress)

Second-year team project (6 students): a handheld scanner that maps a room in 2D and 3D, from a custom PCB built around an STM32 and the VL53L9CX LiDAR to the visualisation app.
Published

November 2026

Note

Work in progress. This page covers the scope and the first steps; it will be updated as the project moves forward.

At a glance

Context Second-year multidisciplinary project, ENSEA, 2026–2027
Team 6 students, supervised by a faculty advisor
Sensors ST VL53L9CX multizone LiDAR, ST LSM6DSV16X IMU
Tools STM32CubeMX, STM32CubeIDE, C (HAL), KiCad, Git/GitHub

Goal

Design a portable, self-contained 3D/2D scanner that measures a room and displays its geometry in real time. The fictional use case is an architecture firm or real estate agency that wants to survey a room quickly and generate a 2D floor plan and a 3D model.

The project covers the whole chain:

  • Electronics: custom PCB built around an STM32 microcontroller and the VL53L9CX LiDAR, from schematic capture to board bring-up.
  • Embedded software: C firmware to configure the sensors, acquire depth data in real time and manage communication.
  • Artificial intelligence: recognition of room elements (walls, doors, windows, obstacles), on the device or offloaded to the computer.
  • Application: data reception (USB, Bluetooth or Wi-Fi) and display of the 2D floor plan and 3D point cloud.
  • Mechanics: ergonomic enclosure designed in CAD and 3D-printed.

Constraints

  • STMicroelectronics components only.
  • Budget of about €150 for the whole project.
  • Battery-powered, with a low-power design.
  • Handheld device: lightweight and easy to use.

Approach

The team works in agile sprints and keeps a lab notebook (decisions, justifications, failure analysis). To limit risk, the priority is a minimum viable product: 2D mapping and raw LiDAR data transmission first, then AI and 3D rendering. Each sensor is validated on a Nucleo development board before the PCB is manufactured.

Progress

IMU validation. I brought up the STEVAL-MKI227KA evaluation board (LSM6DSV16X) on a NUCLEO-F410RB over I2C:

  • I2C bus scan and sensor identification through its WHO_AM_I register;
  • accelerometer and gyroscope configured at 30 Hz by reading and writing registers directly with the HAL;
  • acceleration, angular rate and temperature streamed over the serial port, with a wiring and troubleshooting guide for the team.

Next steps: VL53L9CX LiDAR integration, then PCB design.
